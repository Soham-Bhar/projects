[1.0] Abstract

Six laboratory experiments and three computational studies, drawn from the physics sequence at NUS. The emphasis throughout is on doing the numerical work by hand before reaching for a library, and on carrying uncertainty all the way through to the quoted result rather than stopping at a central value.

Two things distinguish this collection from a folder of submitted coursework. First, the raw data is included: spreadsheets, instrument exports, simulation input decks. Second, the analysis that turned that data into each figure is included as well. A reader who doubts a number can follow it back to the measurement.

[3.0] Selected work

Driven Quadruple Pendulum

[3.1] PC3236 Computational Methods in Physics · Python, Jupyter, Typst

Four coupled masses with no closed-form solution, integrated with a hand-coded RK4 scheme at a fixed 1 ms step rather than a library solver, so the integration behaviour stays open to inspection. The 4x4 mass matrix is solved by LU decomposition at every stage. Validated against SciPy’s adaptive RK45 and agreeing to within one microradian over a 20 s trajectory, then characterised through Poincaré sections, bifurcation diagrams and Lyapunov divergence.

Quantum Bouncer

[3.2] PC2130 Quantum Mechanics 1 · Python

A particle in a linear gravitational potential above a hard floor, one of the few systems with an exact solution in Airy functions. Energy eigenvalues taken from the zeros of the Airy function, a Gaussian packet decomposed onto that eigenbasis, and time evolution by resumming phase factors, so the propagation is exact rather than a finite-difference approximation. The packet spreads, decoheres into an interference pattern with no classical analogue, and later reassembles as a quantum revival.

Interaction of Ions with Matter

[3.3] PC3193 Experimental Physics II · SRIM, Python

Twenty-five Monte-Carlo binary collision simulations of ion implantation, covering hydrogen, boron, argon and gold into beryllium, silicon and gallium arsenide across six orders of magnitude in energy. Ion ranges, damage profiles and sputtering yields extracted by scripted analysis. Every TRIM.IN input deck is published, so any figure in the report can be regenerated from scratch.

[5.0] Reproducibility

Fourteen CSV runs sit behind the Hall effect report, including zero-field and zero-current controls. Four workbooks sit behind the X-ray diffraction analysis. Forty simulation input decks sit behind the SRIM study. The bulk per-ion collision dumps are not published, since SRIM regenerates them from the decks, but everything needed to do so is here.

Where the analysis was carried out in Excel, the workbook is published with its plots and fitting intact rather than reduced to a table of results.
